Think about the media you grew up with.

A show ends. A chapter ends. A movie has credits. Every one of these had a built-in pause — a moment where the content itself asked you: do you want to continue?

Your phone doesn't do that anymore.

Researchers who study interface design have a name for this: they call platforms like this "not a neutral design" — because removing the pause isn't an accident of good UX, it's a specific design decision, one that measurably increases how long people stay by lowering the mental effort it takes to keep going.

There's no page to finish. No credits. No natural moment where the app hands the decision back to you.

That gap — the missing stopping point — is where four specific features live.
